The rulebook tells you what your character can do. It does not tell you what
Friday night actually feels like, or where you sleep, or when you eat. That is
community knowledge, passed player to player for years, and this page is that
knowledge written down.

## The weekend, roughly

Events run four times a year — roughly March, May, August, and November —
Friday evening through Sunday afternoon, at one of two sites in Virginia that
alternate through the year. **Camp Horizons**, near Harrisonburg, hosts the
season-opener and season-closer and has rustic cabins at the cold end of the
year, so pack for cold regardless of what the forecast promises.
**Prince William Forest Park** hosts the two events in
between and has no air conditioning, so it tends to be the hot ones. Prince
William books its LARP dates through a park-wide lottery shared with other
groups, so even staff often do not know those exact dates until after New
Year's — check the official site rather than assuming a pattern.

Friday starts later than you might expect. The supply trailer usually does not
arrive until the early-to-mid afternoon, and nobody is allowed on site until
setup crew has gotten in ahead of everyone else, so check-in tends to be
mid-afternoon. "Game on" — the moment the world starts moving around you — has
crept later over the years and now often lands somewhere around 10 PM to
1 AM, sometimes later. Bring something to do quietly while you wait, and do
not read a late start as a bad sign; it is close to normal.

Saturday is the heart of the weekend, and it typically opens with a short
**new-player orientation mod** right after opening ceremonies — a walk-through
of the mechanics in practice, aimed squarely at people in their first event or
two. Go to it even if you read every rule ahead of time; doing a thing and
reading about it are different skills.

## Town life and mods

Most of what happens over a weekend is **mods** — small adventures, usually
built for somewhere around ten to fifteen people, running in parallel all
over the site all weekend long. Think of the game less like a movie with one
plot everybody follows and more like an MMO: dozens of things are happening at
once, you will only ever see a slice of them, and that is by design, not a
sign you are missing out on something you were supposed to catch. A handful
of larger **field fights** — the weekend's set-piece battles, combat and
non-combat pieces both — punctuate the smaller mods, usually two to four over
a weekend.

Meals and cabin time are treated as real breaks, not something to apologize
for. Mods are never scheduled over mealtimes or run into cabins, so stepping
away to eat, sleep, or just sit down is expected of everyone, new player and
ten-year veteran alike. If you need to step out of the story for a second —
to ask a real-world question, deal with a real-world problem, or just
breathe — the phrase for it is **"you're speaking madness"** (or "may I speak
madness?" to ask permission first). Say it, get your OOC moment, and step back
in. Cabins and the meal table are treated as informal gray space where nobody
expects you to stay fully in character.

For the actual out-of-game safety calls — Caution, Clarification, Emergency,
the Lookdown, and the OK Check-In — see
[Etiquette & Safety](/mechanics/etiquette-safety/); that page is the
rulebook's own account of them and worth reading before your first event.

## Food and sleeping

Only Camp Horizons offers catering (breakfast and dinner, or a bundled meal
plan), and even there it is optional. Prince William has no catering at all,
so most people bring a cooler, split a grocery run with cabinmates, or make a
short supply trip into town. The tavern building usually has a real kitchen —
stove, sink, fridge, microwave — and if you cook in it, bring your own
cleaning supplies and expect it may be informally claimed by whoever is
running the weekend's tavern food.

Nobody tents. You are in a cabin at both sites, and cabin assignments are
handled through the event's registration system and can shift close to the
event — one longtime NPC describes it as "cheerfully chaotic," and that is
the right amount of concern to have about it. Camp Horizons cabins are genuine
depression-era buildings with no air conditioning, heaters that are more of a
suggestion than a guarantee, and thin mattresses, so a foam sleeping pad and a
warm bag matter more than you would think. Prince William's cabins are
similarly rustic but share some more modern common buildings.

## Monster Camp, for anyone curious

If you spend any part of the weekend as an NPC, you will end up in or near
**Monster Camp**, the staging area where NPCs gear up between roles. It has
its own culture: a shared stock of loaner weapons and armor (swords are
plentiful; polearms and spears are limited and have historically been offered
to new players first, specifically to help retention), a running supply of
homemade treats other NPCs bring in, and a genuine habit of checking on each
other after a hard fight or a rough scene. NPC meals are covered by the game
as thanks for volunteering. See [NPCing](/new-to-numina/npcing/) for what it
is actually like to spend a weekend on that side of the story.

## The one thing everyone says

Across years of this community's own chat, one sentence shows up more than
any other, from veterans and staff alike: **nobody knows everything, even
after years of playing.** One player put it plainly — "I've been playing for
two years and still don't know what's going on" — and meant it as reassurance,
not complaint. You are not supposed to see the whole plot in one weekend. Go
to your mods, eat your meals, sleep when you're tired, and let the rest of the
world keep happening without you. It will still be there next event.
