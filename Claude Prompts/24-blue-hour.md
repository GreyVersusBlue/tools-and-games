# 24 — Blue Hour

You are working on Blue Hour, a first-person three.js mountain trail at dusk on
greyversusblue.com. It is the deliberate sibling of Golden Hour (prompt 08) — blue hour is the
cold dusk that follows golden hour, and this is the walk you take after that one. Purely
atmospheric, no score, no fail state, tagged `Explore`. This prompt is self-contained.

## House rules for every file in this repo

- **No build step.** Static files served by GitHub Pages from the repo root at
  `greyversusblue.com`. Plain ES modules through an import map, no bundler, no transpiler, no
  runtime npm dependency.
- **Zero offsite requests.** This piece has never made one. Every texture is drawn into a
  canvas at load and every sound is synthesized in Web Audio. There are no asset bytes at all
  outside `libs/`. Don't regress that — it is a large part of the piece's identity.
- **Each project vendors its own copy of three.js; nothing is shared across projects** (locked
  decision #17).
- **`js/field.js` imports nothing.** It is the mountain as arithmetic — heightfield, trail
  curve, creek, and the seeded deterministic layout of everything standing on it — and
  `test/smoke.mjs` runs it under bare Node with no browser and no three.js. Keep it pure. Every
  new piece of world geometry that anything else needs to agree about goes here.
- **`camera.rotation.order` must be `YXZ`**, not the Euler default. The default decomposes the
  same quaternion into an x/y that are not pitch and yaw once both are non-zero, and anything
  reading `camera.rotation.y` as facing gets nonsense — including the shared drivers'
  `camState`. Golden Hour was bitten by this; `controls.js` already sets it. Don't revert it.
- **A check that only prints is a check that gets ignored** (locked decision #13). The one
  deliberate exception in this project is the OPEN FINDING group in `test/browser.mjs`, which
  prints the summit measurements rather than asserting them — see "What is actually here".
  Convert them to assertions when the summit is fixed.
- **Verify a guard-rail by reintroducing the bug it guards** (locked decision #34).
- **A real-time movement or physics assertion failing under this environment's software-
  rendered Chromium is inconclusive, not confirmed** (locked decision #53). This hits this
  project hard: `test/browser.mjs` measures ~1.7 fps under swiftshader, and `main.js` clamps
  `dt` to 0.1 s, so the world runs at roughly a tenth speed. The suite scales its one timing
  assertion by the measured frame rate for exactly this reason. Never hard-code a walk distance
  here.
- **Measure before deciding something is too heavy** (locked decision #42). This project's
  numbers are 35 draw calls and 280k triangles against a budget of 300 calls.
- **Windows is the dev machine.** Absolute `import()` paths need `pathToFileURL`.

## What is actually here

Eleven `js/` modules, a `libs/` with `three.module.js` + `three.core.js`, `css/style.css`, and
a two-part `test/`. Tagged `Explore`. **No save, no score, no fail state** — and unlike Golden
Hour, no journal, no interaction verbs and no photo mode either. Whether that stays true is the
open question of this project, not a settled decision in either direction.

**860 m of switchback trail** climbing a fog-bound mountainside to a lookout above the cloud
line, benched into the hillside and blended into the forest floor in the ground shader, marked
by 14 blazed posts.

**~3,834 trees in two tiers** — instanced conifers with vertex-shader wind and birches near the
trail, silhouette cards where the fog has already flattened everything anyway — plus ferns,
shrubs and dead grass as billboarded cutouts. All of it seeded and deterministic out of
`field.js`.

**A creek with a footbridge and a waterfall.** The bridge deck *is* ground inside its rectangle
(`walkHeight` returns the deck over the creek), the same mechanism Golden Hour's pier uses.

**Seven cairns**, a ranger cabin with one lit window, deer that freeze before they bolt,
perch-hopping birds, crows, a squirrel, an owl, a fox, and an elk you only ever hear.

**The weather breathes.** Two slow overlapping sine periods (211 s and 337 s) that never line
up, driving visibility from ~100 m down to ~45 m and back. Everything the fog touches — density,
palette, light level, exposure, the shafts, how far the birds sound — reads from that one
number, set in one place, for the same reason Golden Hour sets everything solar from one
elevation: split across call sites it rots. **The fog is not a culling strategy** — measured,
the draw cost is identical in the thickest fog and the clearest. It is mood and depth-precision
headroom.

**The woods are not honest.** `dread.js` is a scheduler of directed beats — a branch breaking
where nothing is, footsteps that continue a half-second after yours stop, the birds all going
quiet at once, eyes low in the treeline, a dark shape up the trail that is gone when you look
twice. Long cooldowns, never the same beat twice running, visual beats gated on fog thick
enough to half-take them back, and nothing above the fog line, where the mountain is honest.
Nothing here can hurt you, which is not the same as nothing being here. **Keep it that way** —
this piece has no threat and should never grow one.

**A debug hook, `?debug` → `window.__bh`**: `setWeatherT`/`getWeatherT`/`fogT`/`altT`,
`teleport`/`face`/`pos`/`surface`, `cairns`/`layout`, `trail`/`yawAlongTrail`, `fireDread`,
`dread`, `info`. Nothing in the piece opens any of these doors itself. Use `yawAlongTrail` to
aim the walker — the trailhead sits at z 145 with `BOUNDS.maxZ` at 150, so a guessed "face +z
and hold W" walks into the edge of the world 5 m later and looks exactly like a movement bug.

**`test/smoke.mjs`, 46 checks**, pure Node, no browser. **`test/browser.mjs`, 25 checks**, real
Chromium through `?debug`. Both exit non-zero on failure. `browser.mjs` needs `playwright-core`
and a Chromium on disk (`CHROME=` overrides the path); neither is a dependency of the piece.

**The summit payoff does not render, and it is the top open item.** The README promises "thin
bright air over a cloud sea"; what renders is a black hillside under a pale sky. Two measured
causes: `atmosphere.js` puts the cloud sea at y = 46 while the summit is a *plateau* averaging
~60 m for 200 m around, so the ring is inside the mountain (100% buried at r = 40, 60% still
buried at r = 200); and above the fog line nothing lifts the ground colour any more, because
the fog was doing all that work and the forest floor has no alpine treatment — lower-half mean
frame luminance at the bench is 6–14/255 against 22/255 on the trail below. `browser.mjs`
prints both as OPEN FINDING notes rather than asserting them.

## Your task

1. **Settle the summit.** It is the payoff of an 860 m climb. Two honest routes: give the
   mountain a real peak so the ground falls away and the sea plane clears the terrain — which
   costs a `smoke.mjs` golden-height rebaseline and is the bigger job — or drop the cloud sea
   and rewrite the promise, ending the walk *in* the fog rather than above it, which is
   arguably truer to the piece's own thesis. Either way the ground shader needs a treatment
   above the fog line so the view is legible at all. Turn the two OPEN FINDING notes into
   assertions when it lands.
2. **The direction question.** Golden Hour grew a journal, hands, photo mode and rare events.
   This piece has none of them. Three defensible answers — parity, deliberate abstinence
   (write it into this file as a locked decision so no future session "fixes" it), or push
   further into what the piece is already best at (more in `dread.js`, a darker second half,
   the summit costing something). Do not pick this one unilaterally if Devon is reachable; it
   is a question about what the piece *is*.
3. **A real GPU run.** Every number on this project is swiftshader. The geometry budget is
   comfortable, but this piece leans hard on large transparent billboards in fog — ferns,
   silhouette cards, mist, shafts — which is fill-rate cost that software rasterization reports
   very differently from a real GPU. Nobody has measured the actual frame rate of this piece
   anywhere.
4. **A touch playtest on real glass.** The hold-the-bottom-third-to-walk scheme has never had a
   thumb on it.
5. **An hour on the trail with ears on.** The dread cooldowns (55–100 s, first beat at 70 s)
   and the fog periods are unheard guesses. They want a real walk, not a scrub.
6. **If your own pass turns up something new**, add it here.
