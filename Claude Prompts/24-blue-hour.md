## House rules for every file in this repo

- **No build step.** Static files served by GitHub Pages from the repo root at
  `greyversusblue.com`. Plain ES modules through an import map, no bundler, no transpiler, no
  runtime npm dependency.
- **Zero offsite requests.** This piece has never made one. Every texture is drawn into a
  canvas at load and every sound is synthesized in Web Audio. There are no asset bytes at all
  outside `libs/`. Don't regress that — it is a large part of the piece's identity.
- **Each project vendors its own copy of three.js; nothing is shared across projects** (locked
  decision #17).
- **`js/field.js` imports nothing.** It is the mountain as arithmetic — heightfield, trail
  curve, creek, and the seeded deterministic layout of everything standing on it — and
  `test/smoke.mjs` runs it under bare Node with no browser and no three.js. Keep it pure. Every
  new piece of world geometry that anything else needs to agree about goes here.
- **`camera.rotation.order` must be `YXZ`**, not the Euler default. The default decomposes the
  same quaternion into an x/y that are not pitch and yaw once both are non-zero, and anything
  reading `camera.rotation.y` as facing gets nonsense — including the shared drivers'
  `camState`. Golden Hour was bitten by this; `controls.js` already sets it. Don't revert it.
- **A check that only prints is a check that gets ignored** (locked decision #13). The one
  deliberate exception in this project is the OPEN FINDING group in `test/browser.mjs`, which
  prints the summit measurements rather than asserting them — see "What is actually here".
  Convert them to assertions when the summit is fixed.
- **Verify a guard-rail by reintroducing the bug it guards** (locked decision #34).
- **A real-time movement or physics assertion failing under this environment's software-
  rendered Chromium is inconclusive, not confirmed** (locked decision #53). This hits this
  project hard: `test/browser.mjs` measures ~1.7 fps under swiftshader, and `main.js` clamps
  `dt` to 0.1 s, so the world runs at roughly a tenth speed. The suite scales its one timing
  assertion by the measured frame rate for exactly this reason. Never hard-code a walk distance
  here.
- **Measure before deciding something is too heavy** (locked decision #42). This project's
  numbers are 36 draw calls and 290k triangles against a budget of 300 calls.
- **Windows is the dev machine.** Absolute `import()` paths need `pathToFileURL`.

## What is actually here

Eleven `js/` modules, a `libs/` with `three.module.js` + `three.core.js`, `css/style.css`, and
a two-part `test/`. Tagged `Explore`. **No save, no score, no fail state** — and unlike Golden
Hour, no journal, no interaction verbs and no photo mode either. Whether that stays true is the
open question of this project, not a settled decision in either direction.

**860 m of switchback trail** climbing a fog-bound mountainside to a lookout above the cloud
line, benched into the hillside and blended into the forest floor in the ground shader, marked
by 14 blazed posts.

**~3,834 trees in two tiers** — instanced conifers with vertex-shader wind and birches near the
trail, silhouette cards where the fog has already flattened everything anyway — plus ferns,
shrubs and dead grass as billboarded cutouts. All of it seeded and deterministic out of
`field.js`.

**A creek with a footbridge and a waterfall.** The bridge deck *is* ground inside its rectangle
(`walkHeight` returns the deck over the creek), the same mechanism Golden Hour's pier uses.

**Seven cairns**, a ranger cabin with one lit window, deer that freeze before they bolt,
perch-hopping birds, crows, a squirrel, an owl, a fox, and an elk you only ever hear.

**The weather breathes.** Two slow overlapping sine periods (211 s and 337 s) that never line
up, driving visibility from ~100 m down to ~45 m and back. Everything the fog touches — density,
palette, light level, exposure, the shafts, how far the birds sound — reads from that one
number, set in one place, for the same reason Golden Hour sets everything solar from one
elevation: split across call sites it rots. **The fog is not a culling strategy** — measured,
the draw cost is identical in the thickest fog and the clearest. It is mood and depth-precision
headroom.

**The climb makes it worse, and that is the whole thesis.** `altT` (the 46→62 m altitude blend)
used to promise relief: fog thinning to 0.006, light coming up, mist and shafts clearing, a
cloud sea. Every one of those terms now runs the other way — the summit is the thickest air on
the mountain at 0.055, the light goes out of it, and the mist gets heavier. `dread.js` reads the
same band and *opens* its gates on it, where it used to close them. **Do not restore the old
gradient.** This is a piece about being somewhere you want to leave; a scenic reward at the top
of the climb contradicts everything below it. The one term that rises with altitude is ambient
light, and only because without it the near ground renders black — that is legibility, not
relief, and `test/browser.mjs` pins it at a luminance floor.

**The woods are not honest.** `dread.js` is a scheduler of directed beats — a branch breaking
where nothing is, footsteps that continue a half-second after yours stop, the birds all going
quiet at once, eyes low in the treeline, a dark shape up the trail that is gone when you look
twice. Long cooldowns, never the same beat twice running, visual beats gated on fog thick
enough to half-take them back. Nothing here can hurt you, which is not the same as nothing
being here. **Keep it that way** — this piece has no threat and should never grow one. Nothing
chases, nothing closes, nothing touches the walker.

**Somebody is in the fire lookout.** The one structure at the top, the one thing on the
mountain that implies other people, and there is a figure at the platform rail that turns to
face the walker wherever they go. It never moves from the platform and never comes down. It is
the single exception to the piece's own vanishing rule — looking away and back does not clear
it — and it buys that exception by never resolving: capped at half opacity, denied by fog, and
gone from the rail entirely once the walker reaches the foot of the tower. `dread.js` owns it.
Do not give it a face, an animation, or a second location.

**A debug hook, `?debug` → `window.__bh`**: `setWeatherT`/`getWeatherT`/`fogT`/`altT`,
`teleport`/`face`/`pos`/`surface`, `cairns`/`layout`, `trail`/`yawAlongTrail`, `fireDread`,
`dread`, `info`. Nothing in the piece opens any of these doors itself. Use `yawAlongTrail` to
aim the walker — the trailhead sits at z 145 with `BOUNDS.maxZ` at 150, so a guessed "face +z
and hold W" walks into the edge of the world 5 m later and looks exactly like a movement bug.

**There is music now, and it obeys the weather** (session 3). A drone in D minor inside
`audio.js` — fades in over 35 s, thickens with fog, and sours with altitude: an E♭2 beats at
~4.4 Hz against the root above the fog line, a tritone joins near the summit and leans harder
while the lookout figure is watching (`watched` in the `audio.update` state). A lonely horn
phrase every 70–140 s, generated by the pure exported `motifPhrase()` — always resolving
downward, phrygian above the fog line — held to the scale by the smoke suite. Every dread
stinger ducks the music to 15% with a slow recovery; the silence beat holds it down. All of it
routes through the master/fog filter, so mute and murk apply. `__bh.music()` reads the live
targets.

**`test/smoke.mjs`, 52 checks**, pure Node, no browser. **`test/browser.mjs`, 32 checks**, real
Chromium through `?debug`. Both exit non-zero on failure. `browser.mjs` needs `playwright-core`
and a Chromium on disk (`CHROME=` overrides the path); neither is a dependency of the piece.

**The mountain has no peak, and you should know that before you touch it.** `mountainH` is a
linear ramp in `z` alone — height depends only on how far up the map you are, the slab keeps
climbing past the trail's end at z = -100 to `BOUNDS.minZ` at -120, and there is no lateral
shape beyond noise. The "summit" is just the point where walking stops. This was diagnosed,
costed and deliberately **not** fixed: the fog now closes at the top instead, so nobody can see
far enough for it to matter. If some future session wants a real view from up there it needs a
peak term in `mountainH` and a `smoke.mjs` rebaseline — and it should read why the view was
removed before putting one back.

Related, also unfixed, also hidden by the fog: `trailYof(1)` returns 65 while the raw hillside
there is ~60, so the last stretch of trail rides a ~5 m berm. Invisible now. Real the moment
anyone lifts the weather at the summit.

## The north star (Devon, session 3)

Devon asked what this piece becomes with no ceiling — full engine, full team, past HTML — and
then set the doctrine that binds even at this scale. Recorded here so every session pulls the
same direction. None of it is a commitment to build; it is the answer to "which way is up",
and it settles the direction question below: **dread, not collection.**

### The dream version, so you know what the prototype is a miniature of

A 3–4 hour first-person narrative piece, Firewatch's lineage but colder. **You are the
relief**: the keeper in the fire lookout is three weeks past the end of their rotation, the
radio goes unanswered, and you are sent up with a pack, a key, and orders to collect the
logbook. The 860 m becomes eight kilometres; blue hour never advances; there is still no sky.

- **The cairns are keepers' cairns** — the tradition is that every lookout who finishes a
  season builds one on the way down. Seven cairns. The logbook pages name eight keepers. The
  whole dread system is authored by that gap: the phantom footsteps always *descend*, the
  shape in the trees always faces *downhill* — everything on this mountain is trying to leave
  except the thing at the top.
- **The logbook's middle entries** mention something circling the tower at night. The later
  entries get calmer — not because it stopped, but because the keeper stopped minding. Madness
  or peace; the game never rules.
- **The ending**: the cab is warm, tea steaming, empty. The last logbook entry is in
  handwriting that has been slowly becoming yours across every page found on the climb. Below,
  at the trailhead, a new walker starts up the first switchback — and the controls now only
  let you watch from the rail. Credits when you look away.
- **The asynchronous truth** (the one feature that genuinely needs the engine leap): every
  walker on the mountain is real. Other players' sessions play back as the phantom steps; the
  shape between the trees is someone mid-climb in their own fog who sees *you* as the shape;
  the figure at the rail is whoever summited before you. The dread was never dishonest — the
  mountain is full of people, every one of them alone. Players work this out together, slowly,
  outside the game.

### The Gone Home doctrine — this part applies NOW, at this scale, in this repo

The player must believe they COULD be in danger the entire runtime, while never once being in
any. Gone Home arms every horror trope and fires none; the fear is 100% real and 0% justified,
and the reveal never mocks it. The mechanism is that the threat is **unfalsifiable in both
directions** — the player can never run the experiment that settles it. Confirm danger once
and fear collapses into mechanics; confirm safety once and it evaporates. Therefore:

1. **Never show teeth, and never show a safe room.** Nothing lunges, ever (already locked) —
   and equally, nothing signals sanctuary. No warm-music moment, no shelter framing. The
   cabin's one lit window must never be voted on: comfort or the worst thing out here, the
   piece stays silent.
2. **Keep the danger plausible, not supernatural.** The wildlife layer is what makes this
   work: deer, elk, fox are demonstrably real animals, so a bear is not a genre element, it is
   a fact about mountains. The player's risk model runs on real-world priors — that's the fear
   doing its own work.
3. **Refuse to acknowledge the experiment.** When a player walks out to where the bear-shape
   stood, there is nothing — and no sting, no cue, no sound of it having left. The scheduler
   must never reward or punish investigation. Unfalsifiable means the game doesn't even
   confirm the question was asked.
4. **The body may be afraid; the game may not agree.** Breath vapour, hesitation, cold — a
   body is allowed. No heartbeat audio, no sanity meter, no pulse vignette keyed to the
   figure: each of those announces "a threat system exists", which is a promise there's a
   threat.
5. **Metadata discipline.** Nothing outside the fiction may leak the answer in either
   direction — board-card copy, README, chip text, achievement-like counters. (The board
   card's "REWARD: THE VIEW ABOVE THE CLOUD LINE" is now technically a lie the mountain tells,
   which is exactly the right kind of copy. Keep writing in-fiction.)
6. **Fear is planted in-fiction and left ambiguous.** If the piece ever grows text (see the
   direction question), the logbook entries above are the template: permission to be afraid,
   never confirmation.

### What can be replicated in HTML, in rough feasibility order

Near-term, inside current architecture and rules:

- **Directional intent in the dread beats**: phantom steps biased to read as *descending*
  (pitch/pan drift down-trail), the treeline shape oriented downhill. Small `dread.js` and
  `audio.js` changes; the eight-keepers story starts existing without a single word of text.
- **An attention director, v0**: `dread.js` already knows camera yaw — extend beat placement
  to spend beats just outside the player's recent gaze arc, and track dwell so a stared-at
  treeline never fires. This is the Alien Isolation director rebuilt for atmosphere, and the
  scheduler is already halfway there.
- **A dead radio**: a prop on the trail or at the cabin, one interaction-free ambient beat —
  squelch and carrier hiss in Web Audio, keyed like the other stingers. Implies the unanswered
  keeper without adding a verb.
- **Bootprints on the last switchback**, already there, ascending only, boot-sized: decal
  quads along the final trail segment, fog-gated. Cheap, wordless, wrong.
- **Tea steam in the tower cab** visible from the bench through the cab glass — one breath-
  style emitter. The cab is warm and nobody says so.

Needs a design decision from Devon first (they add verbs or persistence the piece has sworn
off so far):

- **Logbook pages** as findable props — this is narrative, not collection, but it grows a
  reading verb. The keepers'-cairns naming wants this.
- **Ghosts of your own prior walk** (localStorage replay feeding the phantom-step scheduler) —
  the HTML approximation of the asynchronous truth, but it violates "no save" as currently
  held.
- **A headlamp you choose not to use.**

Waits for the leap: volumetric fog as physics, raytraced acoustics, the live asynchronous
multiplayer, climbing the tower, the handwriting reveal, the ending at the rail.

## Your task

1. **The direction question.** Golden Hour grew a journal, hands, photo mode and rare events.
   This piece has none of them, and the ending pass leaned hard the other way — dread, not
   collection. The remaining defensible answers: keep pushing that way, or write "no save, no
   verbs, no collection" into this file as a locked decision so no future session "fixes" it.
   Parity with Golden Hour is now the odd one out and should not be adopted without asking.
2. **The second half wants more than frequency.** The dread gates open on altitude now, but the
   beats themselves are the same five everywhere on the mountain. Something should change in
   kind, not just in rate, once the walker is above the fog line — the figure in the lookout is
   one answer, and it is currently the only one.
3. **A real GPU run.** Every number on this project is swiftshader. The geometry budget is
   comfortable, but this piece leans hard on large transparent billboards in fog — ferns,
   silhouette cards, mist, shafts — which is fill-rate cost that software rasterization reports
   very differently from a real GPU. Nobody has measured the actual frame rate of this piece
   anywhere.
4. **A touch playtest on real glass.** The hold-the-bottom-third-to-walk scheme has never had a
   thumb on it.
5. **An hour on the trail with ears on.** The dread cooldowns (55–100 s, first beat at 70 s)
   and the fog periods are unheard guesses. They want a real walk, not a scrub.
6. **If your own pass turns up something new**, add it here.
