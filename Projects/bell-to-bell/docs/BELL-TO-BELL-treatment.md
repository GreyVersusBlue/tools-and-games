# BELL TO BELL
### A Creative Treatment — Pie-in-the-Sky Pass

*Working titles considered and rejected for being too on-the-nose: "Do Now," "Room 214," "Contact Hours," "Sustained Silent." Working titles considered and kept in the back pocket: **WITH FIDELITY** (which is what admin says about the curriculum and what the game is secretly about), **PLANNING PERIOD** (ironic — you never get one).*

---

## 0. The One-Sentence Version

You are a teacher with 47 minutes, 28 children, four competing masters, a bladder, and a supernatural ability to see through desks — and the game is about what you spend that on.

---

## 1. Tone Contract

Three rules, and the whole thing lives or dies on them:

**The kids are never the joke.** The *situations* are absurd; the kids are behaving exactly the way twelve-year-olds behave, which is inherently funny without being cruel. A kid asking "is this for a grade" for the fortieth time is funny because of the fortieth, not because the kid is stupid. When a kid is a problem, the game always leaves a door open to the reason — sometimes explicitly (see Withitness, §3.6), sometimes never.

**The teacher is not a martyr.** No violin. The comedy is deadpan, competent, and slightly unhinged in the way of a person on their eleventh year. The player character is *good at this job* and that's the source of both the power fantasy and the horror.

**The bureaucracy is the antagonist and it is not evil, it is just extremely confident.** Nobody in admin is a villain. They are all deeply sincere people implementing a framework they attended a webinar about. This is much funnier and much more true than making the principal a tyrant.

Aesthetic north star: a workplace sitcom shot on a slightly-too-warm fluorescent stock, that once per episode cuts to a two-second superhero sting and then immediately back to a copier jam.

## 2. The Four Meters (and the fifth one you can't see)

The brief proposed Learning / Sanity / Compliance / Vibes. Here's the sharpened version — same skeleton, better teeth, and crucially each one is *someone else's opinion of you* except one:

| Meter | What it actually is | Who's measuring |
|---|---|---|
| **MASTERY** | Whether the kids know the thing. Slow-moving, hard to raise, quietly the point of the game. | The kids' brains (and, badly, the assessment data) |
| **FIDELITY** | Whether you *appear* to be doing it the approved way. Pacing guide, posted objective, the rubric look-fors. | Admin |
| **RAPPORT** | Whether the room gives you the benefit of the doubt. Buys you slack, silence, and second chances. | The kids |
| **BANDWIDTH** | Your finite daily executive function. Everything costs it. It does not regenerate during the school day. | You (nobody else can see it) |

The tensions write themselves and they are all real:

- **Mastery vs. Fidelity.** The pacing guide says you have one day on causes of the Civil War. The kids are *finally* arguing about it. Stay and gain Mastery + Rapport, lose Fidelity, and be behind for the rest of the quarter. This is the game's central recurring decision and it should show up thirty times a year.
- **Rapport vs. Fidelity.** "Do we *have* to do the packet?" You know the packet is bad. Admin bought the packet.
- **Bandwidth vs. everything.** Bandwidth is the real currency. Grading costs it. Withitness costs it. Being nice to a kid who needs it costs it. Answering the email costs *double* if you answer it well.
- **Mastery vs. Bandwidth.** Great teaching is expensive. You can absolutely burn a whole week's Bandwidth on one incredible lesson and then be a zombie Thursday.

**The hidden fifth meter: LEGEND.** You never see a bar for it. It's built from what kids say in the hallway about you, and it accrues from specific memorable events — the day you nailed the demo, the day you let something go and the kid noticed, the day you got the projector working after eight minutes of silent struggle. Legend does nothing this year. Legend is the meta-progression currency (§8) and it's how younger siblings arrive in Year 3 already predisposed to like you. The game never tells you it exists until the end of Year 1, when the credits roll over things kids said about you.

**Bladder.** Yes, it's a real meter. It is the funniest possible resource because every teacher on earth will make an involuntary noise when they see it. It fills across the day, empties only during passing time or planning (if you *spend* your planning on it instead of on copies), and at critical levels applies a hard debuff to Bandwidth and Withitness range. There is a late-game unlock called **THE IRON BLADDER** and it is presented as a legendary artifact.

## 3. WITHITNESS — the centerpiece

### 3.1 The name is the joke and the joke is real

In teacher prep you learn a term from Jacob Kounin's classroom-management research: **withitness** — the teacher's demonstrated ability to know what is happening in all parts of the room at all times, and to communicate that knowing. Every education major has read this sentence. It is a real, cited, academic term that means *literally Predator vision*.

So that's what it's called. The in-game tutorial presents it with a slide that looks like it came from a 2009 PD PowerPoint, Comic Sans header, clip-art of an owl:

> **WITHITNESS (Kounin, 1970)**
> "The teacher's awareness of student behavior in all areas of the classroom at all times."
> *Press [SHIFT] to become aware of student behavior in all areas of the classroom at all times.*

### 3.2 What it looks and sounds like

Activation is a **hard, ugly, glorious tonal break** and it should never stop being funny:

- The fluorescent hum drops out. Everything drops out. There's a half-second of total silence like a movie theater right before the studio logo.
- The room desaturates to a cold blue-grey. Kids render as soft thermal silhouettes — warm bodies, cool desks. Anything *hidden* glows orange-hot through the furniture: a phone under a thigh, a note folded in a fist, a Chromebook tab that isn't the assignment.
- A low sub-bass sine tone. Under it, a *heartbeat* that is yours, and it's a little too fast.
- Directional whisper-audio: conversations you couldn't hear normally arrive as intelligible fragments with a slight radio-comms crackle, panned to their position in the room.
- The HUD is not a HUD. It's a **rubric.** Tells are annotated in the visual language of an observation form — a small box with a checkbox and a sans-serif descriptor, drawn like it was generated by the district's evaluation software:

```
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ ◧ INDICATOR 2.3 — OFF-TASK, COVERT   │
  │   Device engagement, sub-desk         │
  │   Duration: 4:12    Confidence: HIGH  │
  └──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- Deactivation snaps back with an audible *fluorescent buzz* and, always, an ambient classroom sound rushing back in a half-second louder than it should be.
- On very rare occasions the vision comes on and the game plays two seconds of an actual heroic orchestral sting. No comment, no acknowledgment. Then it never mentions it again.

### 3.3 The taxonomy of tells

Tells are tiered by **how hidden** they are, not how bad they are. This distinction matters: the vision is a *perception* tool, not a *morality* tool.

**Tier 1 — Visible to anyone paying attention (no Withitness needed, but it highlights them)**
Head down. Chair tipped. Two kids clearly not doing the thing. The kid with a hand up who's been up for ninety seconds.

**Tier 2 — Standard reveals**
- Phone under the desk, glow through denim.
- The note. Rendered as a hot little origami object with a **projected route line** showing its next three hops. You can see its destination before it does.
- Tab-switched Chromebook (the ghost of a game rendered floating above the screen).
- The whisper conversation, transcribed live in subtitles.
- Wired earbud run up the sleeve, into the hand, propping the head.
- The kid who is copying — a thin orange thread connecting two papers.
- The wind-up: a kid three seconds from flicking a pencil, shown as a faint predictive arc, Angry-Birds style, ending on the back of a specific head.

**Tier 3 — Deep reveals (unlock with Withitness skill or high Rapport)**
- **Rumor threads**: glowing filaments connecting kids, thickness = intensity, showing what information is moving through the room and in which direction. You can see a rumor *arrive* at someone.
- **Pre-Cog Bathroom Sense**: a kid's bladder icon fills in real time. You know who is going to ask, and when. Late-game you can pre-empt: "Go now, before I start." (Massive Rapport gain. Small Fidelity loss for the unstructured movement.)
- **Assignment Ghosting**: the homework a kid didn't do renders as a faint outline on their empty desk.
- **The Hall Pass Economy**: passes glow. You can see one has changed hands. There is an entire underground economy of the laminated bathroom pass and Withitness reveals its ledger.
- **Comprehension aura**: a faint halo indicating who actually got it. Green, amber, red. This one is *devastating* in a good lesson and worse in a bad one, and it's the only tell that hurts to look at.

**Tier 4 — Things you wish you hadn't noticed** (see §3.6)

### 3.4 The cost, and why the cost is the joke

Withitness is limited three ways, and each limit is thematically load-bearing rather than a balancing knob:

**1. It drains Bandwidth, fast.** Noticing everything is exhausting. This is just true.

**2. While you're in it, you are not teaching.** The world *does not pause*. Time keeps running. The lesson keeps needing delivery. So every second in Withitness is a second of Mastery decay and a second where the kids are watching you stand at the front of the room with an intense look on your face, staring at nothing. Extended use triggers ambient dialogue:

> "…is she okay?"
> "He does this."
> "Bro he's buffering."

**3. THE SPIRAL.** This is the good one. Overuse past a threshold doesn't disable the ability — it makes it *worse at its job*. You enter **Hypervigilance**, and the vision starts generating **false positives** with full confidence:

```
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ ◧ INDICATOR 2.3 — OFF-TASK, COVERT   │
  │   Device engagement, sub-desk         │
  │   Confidence: HIGH                    │
  └──────────────────────────────────────┘

  [ You cross the room. It is a granola bar. ]
  [ RAPPORT −4. The class saw. ]
```

Burnout as a *perception* debuff rather than a stat penalty is the single best idea in this document. The exhausted teacher doesn't get slower — they start seeing crime everywhere. Everyone who has taught knows a Hypervigilant colleague. Everyone who has taught has *been* one, in February.

The Spiral has a visual tell: at high Hypervigilance the thermal palette shifts from cold blue toward a hot, sickly red, and the heartbeat gets louder than the whisper audio. The player can learn to recognize their own unreliability. That's a real mechanic and a real feeling.

### 3.5 What you actually *do* with it — The Intervention Menu

Seeing is not the game. Seeing is the *setup*. When you flag a tell, you get a radial of responses, each with a cost, an escalation risk, and a witness effect (does the rest of the room see you do it?):

| Response | Cost | Escalation risk | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Proximity** — just walk over there | Bandwidth (low), lesson tempo | None | The single most effective classroom management technique in existence, and correctly modeled here as free, boring, and undefeated. |
| **The Pause** — stop talking mid-sentence and wait | Tempo | Very low | Room-wide Rapport-neutral pressure. Devastating. |
| **The Name Drop** — work their name into the lesson content without breaking stride | Bandwidth (high — this is a skill) | None | The flex. Legend gain. Requires unlock. |
| **The Nonverbal** — the look, the eyebrow, the tap on the desk without eye contact | Bandwidth (low) | Low | Scales with Rapport. Useless at low Rapport, telepathic at high. |
| **The Public Callout** | Free, feels great | **HIGH** | Fast compliance, Rapport hit, and a real chance of a Blowup (§7) if the kid is having a day you don't know about. |
| **The Quiet Word Later** | Costs your passing time or your lunch | None | Best outcomes. Bankrupts your Bandwidth. The game's most consistent moral: the right move is the expensive one. |
| **Bank It** — say nothing, remember it | Free | — | Adds to your **Leverage** file. |
| **Let It Go** | Free | — | Sometimes the correct play. Sometimes the room notices you noticed and chose not to, which is a *Rapport gain*. |

**Leverage** deserves its own paragraph. Banked observations become a resource you can spend later — in a parent conference ("I've noticed he's been distracted since around the third week"), in a data meeting, in a private conversation that lands harder because you have specifics. But banked Leverage **decays**, and if you cash it in too late it plays as creepy rather than perceptive:

> "I saw you pass that note. In October."
> *[The kid stares. RAPPORT −6.]*

### 3.6 The curveball tier: things you wish you hadn't noticed

This is the tonal spine that keeps the game from being a joke machine, and it should be rare, unannounced, and never commented on by the UI. Withitness reveals what's *actually* happening, and sometimes what's actually happening is:

- The glowing phone under the desk is a text thread with a parent, and the last three messages are about whether they're picking her up today.
- The note being passed, when you intercept it, is a birthday card with eleven signatures on it and a drawing of you with the caption "our teacher (nice)."
- The kid asleep in fourth period is asleep because of a shift that ended at 1 a.m. The tell renders identically to a lazy kid. It is *supposed* to render identically to a lazy kid. That's the point.
- The whisper conversation you tune into is one kid quietly walking another kid through the problem you just explained badly.
- The comprehension aura shows one green halo in a sea of red, and it's the kid nobody, including you, would have guessed.
- A kid is drawing instead of working. The drawing is extremely good.

The game never awards points for these. There's no "Empathy +3." It just shows you and lets the moment sit, and the only mechanical consequence is that the intervention menu that pops up has different options on it, quietly, without explanation.

### 3.7 The other senses

Withitness is one of a small suite, unlocked across years:

- **THE HALLWAY EAR** *(passive, Year 1)* — through the closed door, you hear the specific quality of noise that means something is happening out there. Different audio signature for "a fight," "a spill," "an administrator," and "nothing, they're just like that."
- **ROOM TEMP** *(active, whole-room, no per-kid detail)* — one glance, cheap, gives you an aura reading of the entire class as a single organism. Colors: **Locked In**, **Restless**, **Feral**, **Suspiciously Quiet** (this is the dangerous one), **Post-Assembly**, **Pre-Break**, **Full Moon**. Costs almost nothing. Should be the ability the player uses most.
- **ADMIN PROXIMITY ALERT** *(passive)* — a Metal Gear-style radar ping and a soft *!* when an administrator enters your hallway. Gives you roughly nine seconds to get the objective posted, get the kids seated, and start saying a verb from the standards. Upgrading it extends the warning. Maxed, it distinguishes *walkthrough* from *evaluation* from *just needs the attendance thing.*
- **COPIER SENSE** *(passive, unlocks Year 2)* — from anywhere in the building, you know whether the copier is free, jammed, or occupied by someone running 300 double-sided packets. Presented, correctly, as the most powerful ability in the game.
- **THE VETERAN'S EYE** *(Year 5+)* — you glance at a new teacher and see *their* meters. This becomes the entire late-game (§8).

## 4. Subjects: the same skeleton, wildly different weather

Subject choice doesn't change the systems — it changes the **hazard profile**, the **grading burden shape**, and the **flavor of every event**. Each subject gets a passive, a curse, and a signature minigame.

**SOCIAL STUDIES** — *The Forgotten Discipline*
Passive: **Autonomy.** No state test in most years means less Fidelity pressure and nobody checking your pacing. Curse: **Nobody Checks On You** — also no resources, no budget, and your class is the first thing cancelled for anything. Signature hazard: **The Third Rail** — some units carry a Controversy stat, and a well-taught controversial lesson generates enormous Mastery and a nonzero chance of an email that goes above your head. Minigame: primary source analysis, where you drop a document in front of the room and the room *argues*, and you steer.

**SCIENCE** — *High Ceiling, Open Flame*
Passive: **Lab Day** — a spectacular Mastery-and-Rapport multiplier. Curse: **Safety Compliance** — goggles, MSDS sheets, the eyewash station you must test monthly, and a Hazard meter that, if it tops out, generates an incident report which is a *paperwork boss.* Minigame: **Setup/Breakdown**, a real-time prep puzzle in your 47-minute planning period, because the lab does not set itself up. Recurring event: the class pet. The class pet is a whole storyline.

**MATH** — *Inherited Trauma*
Passive: **The Data Loves You** — you generate the cleanest numbers in the building, and data meetings are trivially easy. Curse: **Baggage** — you start every year with a Rapport penalty you did not earn, from every prior math class the kids have had. Signature line, said aloud by a student in every single unit: *"When are we ever going to use this."* Minigame: **The Board** — a live worked-problem sequence where you must decide whether to fix a kid's wrong answer or build the whole lesson out of it.

**ELA** — *The Mountain*
Passive: **Whole-Child Access** — kids tell you things. Highest Rapport ceiling in the game. Curse: **THE STACK.** Essays are *physical objects that accumulate in your actual 3D classroom.* They pile on the corner of your desk, then the second desk, then a chair. The pile has collision. You can see your grading debt from across the room. At maximum Stack the kids start commenting on it. Minigame: grading a stack against a rubric under a timer, where going fast costs Mastery and going slow costs your entire weekend.

**PE / HEALTH** — *Different Physics Entirely*
Passive: **The Whole Building's Rapport.** Every kid in the school knows you. Curse: **No Room.** You have no walls, no decoration budget, no space that is yours. Your "classroom builder" is an equipment closet and a chart of what happens on a rain day. Signature hazard: **THE INDOOR RAIN DAY**, an escalating containment puzzle. Recurring: the Note From Home, produced with theatrical solemnity, laminated by repeat use.

**ART / MUSIC / ELECTIVE** — *Everything Costs Money and Nothing Is Funded*
Passive: **Sanctuary** — your room is where certain kids come to survive, and you get unique student storylines nobody else gets. Curse: **The Supply Economy** — every material is a consumable with a real cost, tracked to the individual glue stick. Boss fight: **THE CONCERT / THE SHOW**, a full end-of-semester performance sequence. Recurring: your class is cancelled for testing, again. Music sub-hazard: the kiln, the instrument inventory, and the recorder unit, which is a war crime you commit against the entire hallway.

**Proposed additions:**

- **CO-TEACH / SPECIAL EDUCATION** — hard mode with a co-op layer. Two teachers, one room, one lesson plan, and a compatibility stat with your co-teacher that you can raise or destroy. Paperwork load is triple. Rapport ceiling is the highest in the game. IEP meeting minigames are genuinely tense. This is the mode for players who want the *real* one.
- **TEACHER ON A CART** — no classroom. You float between four rooms with a cart. **You do not get a classroom builder.** Your "room" is the cart, and decorating the cart is one of the funniest possible build modes. Everything you need is always in the room you just left. Unlockable, punishing, and beloved.
- **THE LIBRARY / MEDIA SPECIALIST** — technically not a teacher, functionally the person who fixes everyone's projector. Support class. High Legend gain, low Mastery.
- **WORLD LANGUAGE** — passive: you may conduct entire interventions in a language admin does not speak. This is not a joke, it's just very good.

## 5. The Classroom Builder: Sightlines Are the Point

The decoration mode's central move is that **it is not cosmetic.** Your furniture layout literally determines your Withitness coverage. Bookshelves cast **blind spots** — rendered on the floor in the build view as soft grey polygons, like fog of war. The back corner behind the tall shelf is a permanent shadow and the kids find it in approximately four days.

So the build mode is a genuine tactical puzzle dressed as an interior design game dressed as a garage sale.

### 5.1 Currency

- **Your Own Money** (the real budget, tracked to the dollar, and the game keeps a running total across the year that it shows you at the end in a slide titled "Thank you for your service")
- **Points** (school supply points that expire and can only be spent from a catalog of 2007 educational furniture)
- **The Free Pile** — when a teacher retires or leaves, their room's contents become a timed scavenging event. This is the best shopping mechanic in the game. You get eleven minutes and a hand truck.
- **DONORSELECT** — the crowdfunding parody. You write a project pitch (a small writing minigame where you pick a tone: *earnest*, *desperate*, *slyly funny*), post it, and it either funds in three days or sits at 8% for eight months. Funded projects arrive in boxes weeks later with one item missing.

### 5.2 A partial catalog

- **Flexible Seating (wobble stools)** — Rapport +. Chaos +. One will break in November.
- **The Bean Bag** — Rapport ++. Has a stain of unknown provenance. Cannot be cleaned. Kids fight over it every single day, forever.
- **The Emotional Support Mini-Fridge** — Bandwidth regen. Draws power. See: the Fire Marshal event.
- **The Space Heater** — because your room is 61°F. Combined with the mini-fridge and the lamp, trips the hall breaker. This is a real chain of consequences the game models.
- **Lamps instead of overheads** — big Room Temp bonus, small Fidelity penalty ("the room was quite dim").
- **The Classroom Library** — books you paid for. Books do not come back. A slow, permanent, entirely worth-it drain.
- **THE LAMINATOR** — endgame unlock. Presented as a legendary weapon with a slow reveal, particle effects, and a low choral note. Owning your own laminator means never going to the workroom again. It is the single most aspirational item in the game.
- **The Poster** — motivational posters have a **Sincerity** stat and a **Curl** stat. Curl advances over the year regardless of what you do. You will spend real time re-taping.
- **The Rug** — enormous Room Temp bonus. Custodial staff hate it. Relationship stat with custodial staff is real and is the most valuable relationship in the building (§6.4).
- **The Door Decoration** — for the seasonal door decorating contest, which is not optional despite being described as optional.
- **Anchor Charts** — you make them, they're mechanically useful (kids reference them, small Mastery bonus on relevant content), and they accumulate until your walls have no space and you must choose what to take down. *Choosing what to take down is a small act of grief.*
- **The Objective Board** — must be updated daily with today's standard. Costs 30 seconds of morning time. Forgetting it is a guaranteed Fidelity hit if walked through. **Writing it in advance for the whole week is a legitimate strategy and the game rewards you for it.**

### 5.3 The Seating Chart

Its own screen, its own puzzle, and the most replayed system in the game. Drag kids into desks. The chart shows:
- **Sightline coverage** from your teaching positions
- **Volatility edges** — red lines between kids who should not be adjacent, discovered through *play*, not given to you
- **Stabilizer nodes** — kids whose presence calms neighbors, worth their weight in gold, and whom you will absolutely overuse until the game gently shows you the cost of always seating the anxious kid next to the loud kid

Change it too often and Rapport drops ("we JUST moved"). Change it too rarely and volatility compounds. The best players re-chart at each grading period and pretend it's routine.

## 6. The Bureaucracy Suite

### 6.1 Full minigames

**THE OBSERVATION** — the boss fight. Announced or unannounced (unannounced ones use the Admin Proximity Alert). You are scored on a rubric with visible **look-fors** that you can satisfy in-lesson: posted objective, higher-order question, wait time, checks for understanding, "student-led discourse." The comedy: the rubric rewards *performing* the look-fors, and you can absolutely stack a fake-great observation by cramming all five into eleven artificial minutes while the actual learning suffers. Mastery down, Fidelity up, and you will feel a real, complicated way about it. The post-conference is a dialogue tree where you must respond to feedback, and the correct-feeling answer is often the costly one:

> **AP Reyes:** "I noticed only about four students spoke during the discussion. Talk to me about your engagement strategy."
> ▸ "You're right — I want to build in more structured turn-and-talks." *(Fidelity +, and you now owe a follow-up)*
> ▸ "Those four were the ones who'd read it." *(honest; Fidelity −; small Legend)*
> ▸ "That's a great point, I'll add it to my growth plan." *(free, hollow, Bandwidth −1 from the small death inside)*

**THE COPIER** — a full rhythm/pressure minigame, and the game's mascot. 7:14 a.m. There is a line. You have 340 pages. The machine has a code you must enter, a tray that lies about being full, and a jam behavior that requires opening doors B, C, and D in a specific sequence indicated by an unhelpful diagram. Behind you, a colleague with four pages waits with visible patience, and letting them cut costs you time but banks **Colleague Goodwill**, a real currency. The bell approaches. The copier is a character and it should have a name.

**THE DATA MEETING** — a card-based defense. You bring your numbers; the facilitator has a protocol with named steps and a timer; you must produce a "root cause" and an "action step" from your hand. Best cards are things like *"Attendance,"* *"The test doesn't measure that,"* and the legendary *"I disaggregated it and the growth is actually there."* Playing an honest card that implicates the curriculum is high-risk, high-Legend.

**THE PAPERWORK TOWER** — inbox management as physical Tetris. Forms arrive with deadlines and hidden dependencies (Form B requires a signature from someone who is out). Clearing it costs Bandwidth. Letting it grow is *fine, actually,* until suddenly it is a crisis. The correct strategy is a low simmer of guilt, which is accurate.

**SUB PLANS** — you write them (a small authoring minigame: how much detail, what to leave out, whether to name the kid who will lie about the seating chart) and then you **do not play the day.** You read an after-action report the next morning. The sub's note is the funniest text in the game and should be written with love:

> *Note left on your desk, in blue ballpoint, on a half-sheet:*
> "Hi! Class went well. 3rd period was chatty. I could not find the projector remote so we did the worksheet instead — I gave them the whole period. Israel was very helpful. Someone took the stapler. There was an issue at the end of 6th but Mr. Delgado handled it. Thank you for the clear plans!!
> P.S. What is the code for the copier?"

**FIRE DRILL / EVACUATION** — roster accountability under a timer, herding 28 kids through a doorway, the kid who is in the bathroom, the clipboard you must remember, and the mandatory posted evacuation map that you do have, somewhere. Comedy: the drill always fires four minutes into the best lesson of the quarter.

**HALL DUTY** — a patrol minigame during passing time. You stand in the doorway (the correct position: half in the hall, half in the room — the game's most authentic single mechanic), greet kids by name for Rapport, and watch for hallway events. Every second here is a second not spent prepping.

**LUNCH — 22 MINUTES** — an explicit resource allocation screen with a countdown. Split 22 minutes among: *eat*, *email*, *copies*, *bathroom*, *talk to a human adult*. You cannot do all of them. Talking to an adult is the highest Bandwidth restore in the entire game and it is the one players will skip, and the game knows that, and around February it will start commenting.

### 6.2 Background flavor (systems, not minigames)

The mandated online compliance modules with the video that cannot be skipped and the quiz you can pass without watching. The all-staff reply-all chain, modeled as a **spreading fire** with a visible thread count that grows every time you check your email, where the correct play is always to do nothing and the temptation to reply-all "please remove me from this thread" is an available, catastrophic button. The sign-in sheet. The new district initiative announced each August, always with a three-word name and an acronym, always replacing last August's, always described as "not one more thing." The mandatory faculty meeting that could have been an email, *about* an email. The pineapple chart. The learning walk. The vertical alignment committee.

### 6.3 The Calendar Bosses

- **Back to School Night** — a social gauntlet: you deliver the same nine-minute speech seven times. Parent dialogue trees. Set your reputation for the year.
- **Parent-Teacher Conferences** — the big one. Speed-dating format, 8-minute timers, and the deep joke: the parents who show up are the parents of kids who are fine. Leverage (§3.5) cashes in here beautifully.
- **The Week Before Winter Break** — a survival mode. All Room Temp readings shift toward Feral. Nothing you plan will work. The correct play, which the game refuses to admit, is a movie.
- **STATE TESTING SEASON** — a two-week stretch where your class is cannibalized for testing rooms, your schedule is replaced by a color-coded matrix, and you proctor in total silence for four hours with no phone, no book, and no bathroom. Rendered as an *actual playable four-hour-compressed silent room.* It is the most conceptually daring thing in this document and it should be about ninety seconds long and absolutely miserable and hilarious.
- **THE FIELD TRIP** — a full self-contained scenario: headcounts, the bus, the chaperone parent who has opinions, one kid who forgot lunch, and a moment at the museum where a kid says something that makes the entire year worth it.
- **THE LAST WEEK** — the inverse of a boss: nothing is at stake, everything is chaos, and it's an epilogue you play.
- **CHECKOUT DAY** — turn in your keys, clear your walls, sign the form with eleven signature lines. Quiet. The credits of the year.

### 6.4 The Faculty

Real relationship stats, real utility:

- **THE CUSTODIAN** — the most important relationship in the building. High standing = your room gets vacuumed, your spills get handled, and you get told things before admin announces them. Costs: not being a slob, and a card at the holidays.
- **THE FRONT OFFICE SECRETARY** — actually runs the school. Grants access to things the principal cannot.
- **THE VETERAN NEXT DOOR** — infinite advice, 60% of it correct, 40% of it a strategy that stopped working in 2011.
- **THE NEW TEACHER** — needs help. Helping costs Bandwidth. Not helping costs something else.
- **THE DEPARTMENT CHAIR** — your buffer against admin, if you feed the relationship.
- **THE TECH GUY** — a myth. Appears only when the ticket is 11 days old.
- **THE COACH WHO ALSO TEACHES A CLASS** — a source of enormous joy and zero grading solidarity.

## 7. Students: Archetypes, Not Stereotypes

Every student is generated from a small set of **needs** and **postures**, not a "type" label. The player learns them the way you learn real kids: by watching. The game never displays a label. Nonetheless, familiar shapes emerge:

- **The Barometer** — whatever this kid does, the room does 40 seconds later. Identifying yours is the highest-value read in the game.
- **The Negotiator** — treats every instruction as an opening offer. "So do we have to do all of them, or…"
- **The Fortieth Time** — "is this for a grade." Always. Ever.
- **The One Who Fixes Your Projector** — silently, without being asked, in eight seconds. Immunity to most consequences by unspoken treaty.
- **The Sleeper** — see §3.6. The game refuses to tell you which kind you have.
- **The Kid Who Is Extremely Funny** — genuinely funny, at the worst possible times, and the hardest management challenge in the game because you *want to laugh.* There should be a hidden stat for how many times you've broken.
- **The Ghost** — perfect attendance, does the work, you cannot picture their face. There's a mid-year event where you realize you have not spoken to them one-on-one since September.
- **The Volunteer** — hand up before the question ends. Managing the Volunteer is how you find out if you're good.
- **The Returner** — a kid who was in your class two years ago who now finds excuses to stop by your room. This is the meta-progression paying off and it should hit like a truck.

### 7.1 Social systems

**RUMOR MECHANICS.** Information propagates through the seating chart and the hallway on a real graph. Rumors have a *subject*, a *distortion value* that grows with each hop, and a *half-life*. Some are about kids. Some are about you. There is a running background rumor about your personal life that mutates all year and you can only ever see fragments of it through Withitness, and the game never resolves it. In May a kid asks you directly and you get one dialogue choice and no way to know if it helped.

**THE HALL PASS ECONOMY.** Passes are transferable objects. They get traded, hoarded, and counterfeited (a kid will make a convincing one). Withitness reveals the ledger. Cracking down has costs. This is a full, silly, functioning shadow economy.

**BLOWUPS.** Not "bad behavior" — an *escalation state* with a visible pre-window. Something happens, the kid's posture shifts, and you have about six seconds where the right intervention de-escalates and the wrong one commits you. The public callout (§3.5) is the classic wrong one. Blowups are never funny and should never be played for laughs; they're the moment the sitcom drops out and you're just a person in a room with a kid who is having the worst day of their week. Handling one well is the highest Legend event in the game and it awards nothing else.

## 8. The Year, and the Years

### 8.1 The shape of a year

Days compress to their **decision points**, not real time. A day is: arrival window → homeroom → 4–6 class periods (each played as 3–5 decisions plus any active minigame) → duty → planning → after school. Weeks aggregate into a **Friday Report**. Grading periods end in the **Grades Due** crunch — a hard deadline, a Stack, and a decision about what "78" means.

**Winning the year** doesn't look like a high score. It looks like a slow slideshow on Checkout Day: your empty walls, the total you spent of your own money, three lines a kid wrote about you on an end-of-year survey, and the state of your four meters rendered as a single sentence.

Endings by meter shape:
- High Mastery, low Bandwidth → **"They Learned a Lot. You Are Not Okay."**
- High Fidelity, low everything → **"Exemplary."** (The most damning ending in the game.)
- High Rapport, low Fidelity → **"They'll Remember You. Admin Has Some Concerns."**
- Balanced, unremarkable → **"You Made It. See You in August."** — and this is the *good* ending, and it should feel like one.

### 8.2 The multi-year meta-arc

- **YEAR 1 — Provisional.** Everything costs double. You have no unlocks. Withitness is short-range and unreliable. A mentor is assigned who is helpful in year one and becomes a character.
- **YEAR 2 — The Year You Actually Know What You're Doing.** Your materials carry over. This is the honeymoon year and it should be *fun*, and the game should let you have it.
- **YEAR 3 — TENURE REVIEW.** A season-long meta-observation. Everything is portfolio evidence.
- **YEAR 5 — THE STUDENT TEACHER.** You now have a second character with their own four meters that you can only affect indirectly, by modeling and advising. Your Mastery, their Bandwidth. This is a whole new game inside the game.
- **YEAR 8 — THE FORK.** Three paths open, and none is scored as correct:
  - **Stay in the room.** Deepen. The Veteran build, with the Iron Bladder and Copier Sense maxed.
  - **Go into admin.** A genuine heel turn — you become the character delivering the rubric feedback, and in a subsequent playthrough the *AI principal is your old build*, with your habits and your phrasings. This is enormously satisfying and slightly haunting.
  - **Leave.** The game does not punish this. There's a short, kind epilogue and the save file closes with a title card that is not sarcastic.
- **YEAR 10 — THE REUNION.** Kids come back. Which ones show up, and what they say, is determined by every Legend event across every prior year — including the ones you never knew were being recorded. The kid you let slide in Year 2. The one you gave the quiet word to. The Ghost.

**Legacy carryover:** in every new year, some incoming students are **younger siblings** of former students, and they arrive with an inherited Rapport modifier and a line of dialogue on day one that references something you don't remember doing.

## 10. Small Things That Belong In This Game

- **The clock.** Above the door, visible from your teaching position, and it does not move when you look at it. Late in a long day, it moves *backward* by one minute. Once. No acknowledgment.
- **Kid voices** are unintelligible Simlish-adjacent murmur, with subtitles doing all the comedy. Only the PA announcements are fully voiced, and they're always slightly clipped at the start so you miss the first word.
- **The chair scrape.** A specific, perfect, spine-level sound. Used sparingly.
- **The HVAC drone** as constant ambient, and its sudden absence — when the system cycles off mid-lesson and the room gets *quieter than it should be* — is used exactly three times a year as a mood cue.
- **You cannot sit down.** There's a chair at your desk. If you sit in it during a class period the game does not stop you, but no teacher who has played this will ever do it, and that's a joke that only lands for the target audience, which is the whole point.
- **The Stapler.** It goes missing in Q1 and it never comes back and there is no quest to retrieve it.
- **Your water bottle** is a Bandwidth item you will forget to fill. The game does not remind you.
- **The end-of-year survey.** Three anonymous student comments, procedurally assembled from your actual play. Two are funny. One is not.

## 11. What This Game Is Actually About

Underneath the copier jokes: it's a game about **attention as a finite resource**, and about the fact that noticing everything is both a superpower and the thing that destroys you. Withitness is the whole thesis in one mechanic. You can see everything in that room. You can't act on all of it. Choosing what to act on — and being honest with yourself about whether you're seeing clearly or just tired — *is teaching.*

The game should be very funny for about eight hours and then, sometime around simulated February, quietly hand you a moment that isn't.
